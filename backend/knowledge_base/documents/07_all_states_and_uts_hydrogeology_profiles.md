# Hydrogeological Profiles, Extraction Dynamics, and Aquifer Status for All 38 States and Union Territories of India

This compendium synthesizes official Central Ground Water Board (CGWB) Dynamic Resource Assessments and National Aquifer Mapping (NAQUIM) data across all 38 States and Union Territories of India.

---

## Northern States and Union Territories

### 1. Punjab
- **Hydrogeology**: Thick Quaternary Indo-Gangetic alluvial sediments (sand, silt, clay, gravel) forming multi-aquifer systems with transmissive unconfined and semi-confined layers up to 300+ mbgl.
- **Extraction Status**: Heavily Over-Exploited (~164% Stage of Extraction). ~75% of assessment units are Over-Exploited.
- **Key Challenges**: Deepening water tables (falling at 0.5–1.0 m/year), tubewell density, energy-intensive centrifugal to submersible pump transitions.
- **Priority Interventions**: Direct Seeded Rice (DSR), crop diversification away from paddy, canal network revitalization, artificial recharge via abandoned injection wells.

### 2. Haryana
- **Hydrogeology**: Alluvial plains of Yamuna and Ghaggar river basins in north and central tracts; semi-arid aeolian sand dunes and Siwalik piedmont in the south/west.
- **Extraction Status**: Over-Exploited (~134% Stage of Extraction).
- **Key Challenges**: Groundwater depletion in Kurukshetra, Karnal, Gurgaon; inland salinity and waterlogging in Sirsa, Hisar, Rohtak.
- **Priority Interventions**: Micro-irrigation (drip/sprinkler), Mera Pani Meri Virasat diversification scheme, sub-surface drainage in saline belts.

### 3. Rajasthan
- **Hydrogeology**: Arid western Thar desert (deep quaternary alluvium, tertiary sandstones, saline brackish aquifers); central Aravalli metamorphics; eastern alluvial plains (Banas, Chambal).
- **Extraction Status**: Over-Exploited (~148% Stage of Extraction). >85% of assessment blocks are Over-Exploited or Critical.
- **Key Challenges**: Extremely low annual precipitation (<300 mm), fluoride contamination, high salinity, deep fossil aquifers with limited recharge.
- **Priority Interventions**: Traditional water harvesting (tankas, khadins, johads), drip irrigation, Indira Gandhi Canal command conjunctive water use.

### 4. Delhi (National Capital Territory)
- **Hydrogeology**: Quaternary alluvium along Yamuna river floodplains; quartzites of Delhi Ridge forming hard fractured crystalline rock.
- **Extraction Status**: Critical to Over-Exploited (~96% Stage of Extraction).
- **Key Challenges**: High urban extraction, historical over-draft in Southwest and South Delhi; localized salinity in Najafgarh belt.
- **Priority Interventions**: Mandatory rooftop rainwater harvesting in municipal building codes, floodplain recharge reservoirs (Palla project), treated wastewater recycling.

### 5. Uttar Pradesh
- **Hydrogeology**: Vast Gangetic alluvial plain with prolific multi-tier aquifer systems (sand layers intercalated with clay aquitards down to 400+ mbgl).
- **Extraction Status**: Semi-Critical to Safe overall (~69% Stage of Extraction), with western UP districts (Meerut, Muzaffarnagar, Saharanpur) exhibiting localized over-exploitation (>100%).
- **Key Challenges**: Arsenic contamination in eastern districts (Ballia, Ghazipur, Varanasi); falling water tables in western sugarcane belt.
- **Priority Interventions**: Rejuvenation of ponds under Mission Amrit Sarovar, check dams in Bundelkhand hard rock zone, arsenic-safe deep tube-wells.

### 6. Himachal Pradesh
- **Hydrogeology**: Intermontane valleys (Kangra, Paonta, Una) with porous alluvial/boulder gravel; fractured Himalayan sedimentary and metamorphic rocks.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe (~35% Stage of Extraction).
- **Key Challenges**: Drying of natural springs (dharras, naulas) affecting rural drinking water; localized stress in Baddi-Barotiwala industrial corridor.
- **Priority Interventions**: Spring-shed management, check dams in lower Siwalik torrents, gravity-fed recharge systems.

### 7. Uttarakhand
- **Hydrogeology**: Bhabhar (highly permeable gravel/boulder unconfined zone with deep water table) and Terai (artesian flowing unconfined/confined aquifers); Lesser/Higher Himalayas.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe (~50% Stage of Extraction). Localized high extraction in industrial Terai belts (Haridwar, Udham Singh Nagar).
- **Key Challenges**: High runoff, drying Himalayan springs, steep hydraulic gradients.
- **Priority Interventions**: Spring rejuvenation, contour trenching, rainwater retention bunds.

### 8. Jammu and Kashmir
- **Hydrogeology**: Kashmir Valley lacustrine Karewa deposits and Jhelum alluvium; Jammu alluvial piedmont; fractured hard rocks.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe (~28% Stage of Extraction).
- **Key Challenges**: Cold arid high-altitude aquifers in Ladakh border; localized iron contamination.
- **Priority Interventions**: Spring protection, gravity-flow piping under Jal Jeevan Mission.

### 9. Ladakh
- **Hydrogeology**: High-altitude cold desert trans-Himalayan moraines, glacio-fluvial gravels, fractured granite.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe (<15% Stage of Extraction).
- **Key Challenges**: Sub-zero freezing in winter, seasonal reliance on glacial meltwater streams.
- **Priority Interventions**: Artificial ice stupas, percolation tanks for glacial melt storage, trench harvesting.

### 10. Chandigarh (UT)
- **Hydrogeology**: Piedmont alluvial deposits of Ghaggar plain.
- **Extraction Status**: Over-Exploited to Critical (~82% Stage of Extraction).
- **Key Challenges**: High municipal draft, urban paving hindering recharge.
- **Priority Interventions**: Deep recharge shafts in open green spaces, rainwater injection wells.

## Western States and Union Territories

### 11. Gujarat
- **Hydrogeology**: Alluvial plains in Central and North Gujarat (Mehsana, Ahmedabad); Deccan Trap basalts in Saurashtra; Jurassic sandstones in Kutch; coastal tertiary sediments.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe overall (~53% Stage of Extraction), but severe over-exploitation in North Gujarat (Banaskantha, Mehsana, Patan > 100%).
- **Key Challenges**: Deep confined aquifers in North Gujarat, coastal seawater intrusion in Saurashtra/Kutch, high fluoride.
- **Priority Interventions**: Sardar Sarovar canal recharge, Sujalam Sufalam check dam networks, subsurface dykes to block coastal ingress.

### 12. Maharashtra
- **Hydrogeology**: ~82% covered by Deccan Trap basalt (layered volcanic lava flows: weathered vesicular zeolitic basalt forming unconfined aquifer; massive fractured basalt forming semi-confined aquifer); Tapi and Wardha-Wainganga alluvium.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe overall (~53% Stage of Extraction). Over-exploitation concentrated in rainshadow districts (Ahmednagar, Jalgaon, Nashik, Solapur, Pune rural).
- **Key Challenges**: Low primary porosity in basalt (weathered zone 5–15 m depth dries up by summer); high sugarcane water demand.
- **Priority Interventions**: Continuous Contour Trenches (CCT), percolation tanks, Jal Yukt Shivar structures, farm ponds, drip irrigation.

### 13. Goa
- **Hydrogeology**: Lateritic plateaus overlying Precambrian crystalline gneisses and schists; coastal coastal sands.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe (~34% Stage of Extraction).
- **Key Challenges**: High monsoon runoff, coastal tidal salinity, seasonal iron/manganese in mining belts.
- **Priority Interventions**: Bandharas (check weirs), rooftop harvesting in urban areas, roof injection.

### 14. Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu (UT)
- **Hydrogeology**: Coastal alluvium and basaltic lava flows.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe (~45% Stage of Extraction).
- **Key Challenges**: Coastal salinity, rapid industrialization draft.

## Southern States and Union Territories

### 15. Karnataka
- **Hydrogeology**: Hard rock crystalline terrain (~98% of state: Peninsular Gneiss, granites, Dharwar schists) with shallow weathered mantle and deeper fractured joint zones down to 200+ mbgl; Deccan basalts in northern districts; coastal laterite.
- **Extraction Status**: Semi-Critical (~68% Stage of Extraction). Over-Exploited blocks concentrated in southeastern districts (Kolar, Chikkaballapur, Tumakuru, Bengaluru Rural).
- **Key Challenges**: Deep tubewell bore drilling (>300 m), high fluoride and nitrate in crystalline belts, urbanization stress.
- **Priority Interventions**: Desilting traditional irrigation tanks, watershed check dams, treated urban water secondary recharge (KC Valley project).

### 16. Tamil Nadu
- **Hydrogeology**: ~73% hard crystalline rock (gneiss, charnockite); coastal sedimentary belt (Cauvery delta alluvium, Cuddalore sandstone).
- **Extraction Status**: Critical to Semi-Critical (~78% Stage of Extraction), with numerous over-exploited firkas in western belt (Coimbatore, Salem, Tirupur).
- **Key Challenges**: High tubewell extraction, erratic northeast monsoon dependence, coastal saltwater intrusion in Minjur/Chennai coastal aquifer.
- **Priority Interventions**: Mandatory rooftop rainwater harvesting (state-wide legislation), check dams, percolation ponds, rejuvenation of Ooranis and temple tanks.

### 17. Andhra Pradesh
- **Hydrogeology**: Consolidated crystallines (granite, gneisses) in Rayalaseema; Cuddapah basin sandstones/limestones; coastal deltaic alluvium of Godavari and Krishna.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe overall (~30% Stage of Extraction), with severe localized stress in Rayalaseema (Anantapur, Chittoor).
- **Key Challenges**: Recurrent drought in Rayalaseema, high fluoride in granite aquifers, coastal aquaculture salinity.
- **Priority Interventions**: Panta Sanjeevani farm ponds, recharge shafts, check dams in dry micro-watersheds.

### 18. Telangana
- **Hydrogeology**: Hard rock Peninsular Gneissic Complex, granites; basalt in Adilabad; Gondwana sandstones along Godavari valley.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe overall (~51% Stage of Extraction). Notable recovery observed following the implementation of Mission Kakatiya.
- **Key Challenges**: High fluoride in Nalgonda, historical deep borewell drilling in central plateau.
- **Priority Interventions**: Mission Kakatiya (tank restoration), Kaleshwaram Lift Irrigation filling of minor irrigation tanks for artificial recharge.

### 19. Kerala
- **Hydrogeology**: Western coastal plains (alluvium and beach sands); midland laterites; eastern crystalline Western Ghats.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe (~51% Stage of Extraction).
- **Key Challenges**: Rapid runoff to Arabian Sea despite heavy monsoon (>3,000 mm), summer water scarcity in lateritic hills, open well drying.
- **Priority Interventions**: Sub-surface dykes, open well recharge using rooftop runoff, contour bunds on slopes.

### 20. Puducherry (UT)
- **Hydrogeology**: Coastal alluvium, tertiary sandstones.
- **Extraction Status**: Critical to Over-Exploited (~75% Stage of Extraction).
- **Key Challenges**: Seawater intrusion into deep tertiary aquifers due to intensive coastal pumping.
- **Priority Interventions**: Desalination, artificial recharge injection wells, strict regulation on coastal borewells.

### 21. Lakshadweep (UT)
- **Hydrogeology**: Coral atolls with a thin freshwater lens (Ghyben-Herzberg lens) floating on denser saline seawater, typically 1–2 meters thick.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe (<60% Stage of Extraction) but extremely fragile.
- **Key Challenges**: Risk of saline upconing from motorized pumping, bacterial contamination in shallow dug wells.
- **Priority Interventions**: Infiltration galleries, Low-Temperature Thermal Desalination (LTTD), bans on deep borewells.

### 22. Andaman and Nicobar Islands (UT)
- **Hydrogeology**: Marine sedimentary rocks, ophiolites, coastal coralline sands, and narrow valley alluvium.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe (<25% Stage of Extraction).
- **Key Challenges**: High rainfall runoff into the Bay of Bengal, dry-season shortages in Port Blair.
- **Priority Interventions**: Ring wells, stream bunds, rooftop rainwater storage.

## Central and Eastern States

### 23. Madhya Pradesh
- **Hydrogeology**: Diverse geology: Deccan basalt in Malwa plateau; Vindhyan sandstones/shales; Gondwana coal-bearing formations; Bundelkhand granite in north; Narmada valley alluvium.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe overall (~58% Stage of Extraction). Over-exploitation concentrated in western Malwa (Indore, Ujjain, Ratlam, Mandsaur).
- **Key Challenges**: Tubewell depletion in soybean/wheat Malwa belt, drought-prone Bundelkhand.
- **Priority Interventions**: Bori bandhs, check dams, Narmada-Kshipra link pipeline conjunctive recharge.

### 24. Chhattisgarh
- **Hydrogeology**: Cuddapah sedimentary limestones and sandstones of Chhattisgarh Basin; Precambrian granites/gneisses; Gondwana rocks in north.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe (~46% Stage of Extraction).
- **Key Challenges**: High iron and fluoride in localized tribal belts, mine dewatering impact.
- **Priority Interventions**: Percolation tanks, rejuvenation of traditional dhabas and ponds.

### 25. Bihar
- **Hydrogeology**: Deep Gangetic alluvium with high transmissivity and massive unconfined to multi-layer semi-confined aquifers.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe (~48% Stage of Extraction).
- **Key Challenges**: Arsenic in 18 districts along Ganga, iron and fluoride in southern marginal districts, summer drying of shallow hand pumps.
- **Priority Interventions**: Piped drinking water schemes under Har Ghar Nal Ka Jal, deep tubewells tapping uncontaminated third aquifer tier (>150 m).

### 26. Jharkhand
- **Hydrogeology**: Chota Nagpur granitic gneiss hard rock plateau; Gondwana sandstones in Damodar valley.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe (~33% Stage of Extraction).
- **Key Challenges**: Poor water retention in hilly hard rock plateau, drying of open dug wells by February-March, coalfield dewatering.
- **Priority Interventions**: Dobhas (small farm ponds), trench-cum-bunding, check dams on seasonal rivulets.

### 27. West Bengal
- **Hydrogeology**: Vast Bengal basin quaternary alluvium in south and central; Terai alluvium in north; hard crystalline rocks in Purulia/Bankura.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe overall (~44% Stage of Extraction).
- **Key Challenges**: Severe geogenic Arsenic contamination in upper aquifers across 8 districts (Murshidabad, Malda, Nadia, North 24 Parganas); coastal salinity in Sundarbans delta.
- **Priority Interventions**: Deep tubewells in arsenic-free lower aquifers (>200 mbgl), surface water supply from Bhagirathi-Hooghly, rainwater harvesting in Purulia.

### 28. Odisha
- **Hydrogeology**: Coastal deltaic alluvium (Mahanadi, Brahmani, Baitarani); Eastern Ghats crystallines and gondwana formations in interior.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe (~42% Stage of Extraction).
- **Key Challenges**: Cyclonic saline storm surges in coastal districts (Kendrapara, Jagatsinghpur), localized fluoride in Nuapada/Nayagarh.
- **Priority Interventions**: Rooftop harvesting, check dams in upstream hilly catchments, saline embankment barriers.

## North-Eastern States

### 29. Assam
- **Hydrogeology**: Prolific Brahmaputra and Barak alluvial plains with massive gravelly/sandy aquifers with shallow water tables (<2 to 5 mbgl).
- **Extraction Status**: Safe (~13% Stage of Extraction - immense untapped potential).
- **Key Challenges**: High geogenic iron (>1.0 mg/L) across 24 districts; localized arsenic; recurrent monsoon floods.
- **Priority Interventions**: Iron Removal Plants (IRPs), community solar tubewells for irrigation, sanitary well protection during floods.

### 30. Arunachal Pradesh
- **Hydrogeology**: Himalayan fractured rocks and piedmont gravel deposits in foothill valleys.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe (<5% Stage of Extraction).
- **Key Challenges**: Hilly terrain, reliance on surface springs and gravity streams.
- **Priority Interventions**: Spring-shed management, community gravity pipes.

### 31. Manipur
- **Hydrogeology**: Intermontane Imphal valley lacustrine/fluvial alluvium; surrounding Tertiary hill formations.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe (<10% Stage of Extraction).
- **Key Challenges**: High iron content, lake catchment degradation.

### 32. Meghalaya
- **Hydrogeology**: Shillong Plateau sandstones, quartzites, and Archaean gneisses; limestone karst in southern slopes (Cherrapunji/Mawsynram).
- **Extraction Status**: Safe (<20% Stage of Extraction).
- **Key Challenges**: Despite world-record rainfall, rapid runoff and karst sinkhole drainage lead to acute winter drinking water shortages; coal acid mine drainage.
- **Priority Interventions**: Traditional bamboo drip irrigation, check weirs in valley bottoms, karst spring protection.

### 33. Mizoram
- **Hydrogeology**: Steeply dipping Tertiary sandstones and shales with high relief.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe (<5% Stage of Extraction).
- **Key Challenges**: Spring drying due to shifting cultivation (Jhum) and deforestation.
- **Priority Interventions**: Spring rejuvenation, community rooftop rainwater storage tanks.

### 34. Nagaland
- **Hydrogeology**: Disang and Barail sandstone-shale ridges and narrow valley gravels.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe (<10% Stage of Extraction).
- **Key Challenges**: Water supply in hill settlements, spring discharge variation.

### 35. Sikkim
- **Hydrogeology**: Complex Himalayan folded crystalline metamorphics, moraines, and gravel terraces.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe (<10% Stage of Extraction).
- **Key Challenges**: Dependence on Dhara springs, glacial lake outburst flood impacts.
- **Priority Interventions**: Dhara Vikas initiative (spring-shed mapping and artificial recharge trenches in recharge zones).

### 36. Tripura
- **Hydrogeology**: North-south trending anticlinal sandstone ridges and synclinal alluvial valleys.
- **Extraction Status**: Safe (~18% Stage of Extraction).
- **Key Challenges**: High iron in shallow groundwater, seasonal dry-period deficits in hilly tracts.
