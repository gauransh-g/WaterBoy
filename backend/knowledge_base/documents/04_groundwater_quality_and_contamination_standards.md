# Groundwater Quality Standards, Geogenic and Anthropogenic Contaminants

## 1. Regulatory Benchmarks (BIS IS 10500:2012)
Groundwater potable suitability in India is governed by the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) drinking water specifications (IS 10500:2012, second revision):
- **pH**: Acceptable limit 6.5 to 8.5 (no relaxation).
- **Total Dissolved Solids (TDS)**: Acceptable limit 500 mg/L; permissible limit in absence of alternate source 2,000 mg/L.
- **Electrical Conductivity (EC)**: Indicator of salinity and mineral dissolution; values $> 1,500\ \mu\text{S/cm}$ impair agricultural and drinking utility.
- **Nitrate ($\text{NO}_3^-$)**: Acceptable limit 45 mg/L (no relaxation due to methemoglobinemia / blue baby syndrome risks in infants).
- **Fluoride ($\text{F}^-$)**: Acceptable limit 1.0 mg/L; permissible limit 1.5 mg/L. Concentrations $> 1.5\ \text{mg/L}$ cause dental and crippling skeletal fluorosis.
- **Arsenic ($\text{As}$)**: Acceptable limit 0.01 mg/L (10 ppb); strictly no relaxation due to severe carcinogenic toxicity and blackfoot disease risks.
- **Iron ($\text{Fe}$)**: Acceptable limit 1.0 mg/L.
- **Lead ($\text{Pb}$)**: Acceptable limit 0.01 mg/L.
- **Uranium ($\text{U}$)**: Atomic Energy Regulatory Board (AERB) provisional guideline limit 30 ppb ($\mu\text{g/L}$).

## 2. Geogenic Groundwater Contamination Hotspots in India

### Fluoride Contamination
Geogenic fluoride leaching occurs predominantly from weathering of fluoride-bearing minerals (fluorite, apatite, biotite) in granitic and gneissic basement rocks under alkaline and low-calcium conditions:
- **Major Affected States**: Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Gujarat, Karnataka, Haryana, and parts of Odisha.

### Arsenic Contamination
Arsenic release is driven by reductive dissolution of iron-oxyhydroxides in Holocene shallow alluvial aquifers along deltaic and floodplain sediments:
- **Major Affected States**: West Bengal (Malda, Murshidabad, Nadia, 24 Parganas), Bihar (Ganga plain districts), Uttar Pradesh, Assam (Barak and Brahmaputra valleys), and Jharkhand.

### Salinity and Coastal Ingress
Occurs due to inherent formation salinity in arid inland basins (parts of western Rajasthan, Haryana, Punjab) or seawater intrusion in over-pumped coastal aquifers (Minjur basin in Tamil Nadu, Saurashtra coast in Gujarat, and coastal Odisha).

## 3. Anthropogenic Contamination
- **Nitrate Pollution**: Widespread across intensive agricultural belts (Punjab, Haryana, western UP) from excessive urea and synthetic fertilizer application, as well as unlined sewage septic tanks in dense peri-urban settlements.
- **Industrial Effluent Ingress**: Heavy metal enrichment (Chromium in Kanpur tanneries and Ranipet, Tamil Nadu; Cadmium, Nickel) from untreated industrial effluent discharge into unlined drains.
