# Master Plan for Artificial Recharge and Rainwater Harvesting in India

## 1. National Context and Master Plan Scope
The Master Plan for Artificial Recharge to Ground Water in India, formulated by the Central Ground Water Board (CGWB), outlines a strategic blueprint to harness surplus monsoon runoff to augment depleted aquifers. Across India, an estimated surplus runoff volume exceeding 180 Billion Cubic Meters (BCM) is non-committed and can be stored in subsurface aquifer formations, minimizing evaporative losses associated with surface reservoirs.

## 2. Hydrogeological Matching of Recharge Structures

### In Hard Rock Aquifers (Basalts, Granites, Gneisses)
Hard rock formations occupy nearly 65% of peninsular India, characterized by low primary porosity and storage limited to weathered zones and secondary fractures:
1. **Check Dams and Nala Bunds**: Constructed across 1st to 3rd order seasonal streams to arrest monsoon velocity, hold water for 3 to 5 months, and facilitate infiltration into the weathered saprolite zone.
2. **Percolation Tanks (PTs)**: Excavated earthen embankments sited on permeable strata upstream of agricultural well clusters. Tank bed desiltation is crucial to prevent clay clogging.
3. **Subsurface Dykes (Groundwater Dams)**: Low-permeability underground masonry or clay barriers built across narrow valley throats in sandy riverbeds to block subsurface lateral drainage and raise upstream water tables without requiring land acquisition.
4. **Recharge Shafts and Pits**: Ideal where impermeable clay or hard cap-rock overlies deeper fractured zones. Gravel-filtered shafts bypass impervious layers to deliver filtered runoff directly into aquifers.

### In Alluvial Aquifers (Indo-Gangetic, Coastal, and River Basins)
1. **Injection Wells**: In thick alluvial multi-layered aquifer systems where deep semi-confined aquifers are desaturated, filtered water is injected by gravity or low pressure into deep slotted casing pipes.
2. **Spreading Basins and Flooding Fields**: Utilized in bhabar and terai piedmont zones where high hydraulic conductivity allows rapid surface percolation.
3. **Canal Bank Recharge**: Intercepting seepage from unlined irrigation channels or diversion canals during monsoon spillage.

## 3. Rooftop Rainwater Harvesting (RTRWH) in Urban and Peri-Urban Areas
With expanding impervious pavements in metropolitan hubs (Delhi, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai, Pune), natural recharge is severely curtailed. CGWB guidelines mandate rooftop rainwater harvesting:
- **Filtering Systems**: Gravel-sand-charcoal filters, dual-chamber leaf traps, or mechanical mesh strainers are mandatory to prevent suspended solids from clogging aquifer pores.
- **Direct Borewell Recharge Protocols**: Runoff from roofs must never be directly introduced into drinking borewells without filtration and first-flush diversion (diverting the first 15 to 20 minutes of rain containing atmospheric fallout and rooftop grime).
- **Urban Bylaws**: Model building bylaws published by the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs mandate RTRWH for all plots exceeding 100 to 300 square meters.
