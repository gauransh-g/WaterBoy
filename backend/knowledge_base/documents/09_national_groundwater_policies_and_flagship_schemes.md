# National Groundwater Policies, Statutory Regulatory Frameworks, and Flagship Schemes

## 1. Central Ground Water Authority (CGWA) Regulatory Guidelines
Under Section 3(3) of the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, the Central Ground Water Authority (CGWA) regulates and controls groundwater development and management across India.

### Key Guidelines (Notified September 2020 & Subsequent Amendments):
1. **Mandatory No Objection Certificate (NOC)**:
   - All commercial, industrial, mining, and infrastructure extraction entities must obtain an online NOC from CGWA/State Ground Water Authorities.
   - Micro and small enterprises extracting < 10 m³/day, agricultural users, and domestic users are exempt from NOC requirements.
2. **Groundwater Abstraction / Restoration Charges**:
   - Extraction fees are levied progressively based on quantum and assessment category: highest charges apply to industries operating in Over-Exploited and Critical assessment units.
   - Funds collected are earmarked for state artificial recharge and groundwater restoration projects.
3. **Mandatory Digital Water Flow Meters & Telemetry**:
   - Entities extracting > 10 m³/day must install tamper-proof ultrasonic or electromagnetic flow meters connected to centralized cloud telemetry.
4. **Mandatory Artificial Recharge Obligations**:
   - Commercial and infrastructure projects must recharge a minimum of 50% to 100% of their annual extraction volume into local aquifers through verified rainwater harvesting systems.

## 2. Flagship National Groundwater Programs

### 1. Atal Bhujal Yojana (ATAL JAL)
- **Financial Outlay**: ₹6,000 Crore (50% World Bank loan, 50% Government of India budgetary support).
- **Core Strategy**: Community-led participatory groundwater management with Disbursed Linked Indicators (DLIs).
- **Target Areas**: 8,350 Gram Panchayats across 80 water-stressed districts in 7 states: Gujarat, Haryana, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, and Uttar Pradesh.
- **Key Interventions**: Preparation of community Water Security Plans (WSPs), construction of water harvesting structures, installation of digital water level recorders, and promotion of micro-irrigation and crop diversification.

### 2. Jal Jeevan Mission (Har Ghar Jal)
- **Mandate**: Provide Functional Household Tap Connections (FHTC) delivering 55 liters per capita per day (lpcd) of potable water to every rural household by 2024–2025.
- **Groundwater Nexus**: ~80% of rural piped supply schemes rely on groundwater sources (borewells/tubewells). ATAL JAL and NAQUIM provide critical source sustainability mapping to prevent tube-well failure.

### 3. National Aquifer Mapping and Management Programme (NAQUIM)
- **Executing Body**: Central Ground Water Board (CGWB).
- **NAQUIM 1.0 (2012–2023)**: Successfully delineated and mapped 25.15 lakh square kilometers of mappable area in India at 1:50,000 scale using advanced heliborne geophysical surveys (Transient Electromagnetic - TEM), exploratory drilling, and hydrogeochemical sampling.
- **NAQUIM 2.0 (2023–2026)**: High-resolution 3D aquifer modeling at 1:10,000 scale focused on water-stressed, urban, and coastal priority zones.

### 4. Mission Amrit Sarovar
- **Target**: Rejuvenation and development of at least 75 water bodies (Amrit Sarovars) in every district of India (~50,000 ponds nationwide).
- **Impact on Groundwater**: Creating local surface retention storage of ~150 to 200 million cubic meters, acting as continuous percolation sources for shallow aquifers.

### 5. Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY) - Har Khet Ko Pani & Per Drop More Crop
- Accelerates command area development and promotes high-efficiency precision irrigation (drip and micro-sprinklers) to curb agricultural groundwater over-draft.
