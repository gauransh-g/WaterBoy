# Central Ground Water Board (CGWB) Ground Water Estimation Methodology (GEC-2015)

## 1. Overview and Institutional Mandate
The Central Ground Water Board (CGWB), under the Department of Water Resources, River Development and Ganga Rejuvenation, Ministry of Jal Shakti, Government of India, is the apex national organization entrusted with assessing, monitoring, and managing India's dynamic groundwater resources. Periodic national assessments are conducted jointly with State Ground Water Departments utilizing the Ground Water Estimation Committee (GEC-2015) methodology.

## 2. Assessment Units and Spatial Granularity
Groundwater resource assessments are carried out at discrete hydrological or administrative units:
- In hard rock terrains (peninsular India, Deccan trap, crystallines): Assessment units typically correspond to watershed or sub-basin boundaries delineated using drainage networks, but are often administratively mapped to Talukas, Tehsils, Blocks, or Mandals for policy implementation.
- In alluvial terrains (Indo-Gangetic-Brahmaputra plains): Administrative blocks or tehsils serve as the primary assessment units.
- In urban areas: Municipal corporation or development authority boundaries are assessed separately to account for high paved fractions and impervious surface runoff.

## 3. Core Estimation Equations and Metrics

### Annual Extractable Ground Water Resource (AER)
The Total Annual Ground Water Recharge is the sum of monsoon season recharge and non-monsoon season recharge. A provision for natural discharge during non-monsoon periods (baseflow to rivers, springs, and subsurface outflows, typically estimated at 5% to 10% of total recharge depending on hydrogeological settings) is deducted to yield the Annual Extractable Ground Water Resource:
$$\text{Annual Extractable Ground Water Resource} = \text{Total Annual Recharge} - \text{Natural Discharge}$$

### Total Annual Ground Water Draft (Extraction)
Total extraction represents the cumulative withdrawal of groundwater for all human uses, categorized into:
1. Irrigation Draft: Estimated based on well census, operational hours of pumping machinery, crop water requirements, or electric power consumption data.
2. Domestic Draft: Calculated from rural and urban census population multiplying per capita consumption standards (e.g., 55 to 135 liters per capita per day).
3. Industrial Draft: Computed from licensed industrial borewell permits and pollution control board records.

### Stage of Ground Water Extraction (SOE)
The Stage of Ground Water Extraction is the paramount indicator of groundwater development stress:
$$\text{Stage of Ground Water Extraction (\%)} = \left(\frac{\text{Total Annual Ground Water Draft}}{\text{Annual Extractable Ground Water Resource}}\right) \times 100$$

## 4. Categorization Criteria for Assessment Units
Assessment units across India are classified into five standard categories based on the Stage of Extraction and long-term pre-monsoon and post-monsoon water level trend analyses (over a 10-year observation baseline):

1. **Safe**:
   - Stage of Extraction $\le 70\%$.
   - No significant long-term decline observed in either pre-monsoon or post-monsoon water levels.
   - Development potential exists for sustainable future extraction.

2. **Semi-Critical**:
   - Stage of Extraction $> 70\%$ and $\le 90\%$.
   - May exhibit significant long-term decline in either pre-monsoon or post-monsoon water levels.
   - Requires cautious monitoring, conservation interventions, and restriction on unmetered new energization.

3. **Critical**:
   - Stage of Extraction $> 90\%$ and $\le 100\%$.
   - Often exhibits decline in both pre-monsoon and post-monsoon water levels.
   - Urgent regulatory oversight and mandatory artificial recharge required.

4. **Over-Exploited**:
   - Stage of Extraction $> 100\%$.
   - Annual extraction exceeds net annual replenishable recharge.
   - Significant long-term water table decline across pre-monsoon and post-monsoon seasons.
   - Severe depletion risks, tube-well drying, aquifer compaction, and baseflow reduction.

5. **Saline**:
   - Assessment units where groundwater in unconfined or principal aquifers is inherently brackish or saline (electrical conductivity exceeding acceptable drinking and irrigation thresholds).
