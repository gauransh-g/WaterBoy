# Major Aquifer Systems, Depth-to-Water Levels, and Seasonal Fluctuations in India

## 1. Principal Aquifer Systems of India (CGWB National Aquifer Mapping - NAQUIM)
India's hydrogeological landscape is classified into 14 Principal Aquifer Systems:

1. **Alluvium (Indo-Gangetic, Brahmaputra, and Coastal Plains)**:
   - Covers ~33% of India's landmass (Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Assam, coastal deltas).
   - Highly porous and permeable sands and gravels intercalated with silt/clay.
   - Specific yield ranges from 10% to 20%; transmissivity often exceeds 1,000 to 4,000 m²/day.
   - Yield of deep tubewells: 100 to 250 m³/hour.

2. **Deccan Trap Basalts**:
   - Covers ~16% of geographical area (Maharashtra, Western Madhya Pradesh, Saurashtra, Northern Karnataka).
   - Layered volcanic lava flows (pahoehoe and aa types). Weathered vesicular zeolitic horizons act as unconfined aquifers (5 to 20 m depth).
   - Fractured jointed columnar basalt acts as semi-confined/confined aquifers.
   - Specific yield: 1% to 3%; low storage capacity leads to rapid summer depletion.

3. **Crystalline Hard Rocks (Granites, Gneisses, Charnockites, Schists)**:
   - Covers ~35% of Peninsular India (Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Odisha, Jharkhand).
   - Devoid of primary porosity; water occurs strictly in weathered mantles (saprolite) and secondary fractures/joints/faults.
   - Specific yield: 1% to 2.5%; yields drop drastically beyond 100–150 meters depth unless discrete fracture zones are intersected.

4. **Sedimentary Sandstones and Shales (Gondwana and Vindhyan Basins)**:
   - Located in Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Rajasthan, Telangana.
   - Moderate porosity in sandstones (5% to 12%); shales act as aquitards.

5. **Limestone and Karst Systems (Cuddapah, Vindhyan, Megahalaya Karst)**:
   - Secondary dissolution cavities, sinkholes, and subterranean conduits.
   - Extreme yield variability ranging from dry holes to prolific cavernous flow (>50 m³/hour).

## 2. Depth to Water Level Ranges Across India (Pre-Monsoon vs Post-Monsoon)
The Central Ground Water Board monitors national water table levels 4 times annually (January, May/Pre-monsoon, August, November/Post-monsoon) through a network of over 23,000 Ground Water Monitoring Wells (GWMW) and automated Digital Water Level Recorders (DWLR):

### 1. Shallow Water Table Zone (< 2 to 5 meters below ground level - mbgl)
- **Geographic Extent**: Brahmaputra valley (Assam), northern Bihar Terai, coastal tracts of Odisha, West Bengal, and Kerala, and canal command areas of northern India.
- **Seasonal Behavior**: Pre-monsoon water level is 3–5 mbgl; post-monsoon rises to <1.5 mbgl or near ground surface.
- **Risk**: Prone to waterlogging and soil salinization in canal commands.

### 2. Moderate Water Table Zone (5 to 10 mbgl)
- **Geographic Extent**: Sub-Himalayan valleys, central Gangetic plain (eastern UP, central Bihar), deltaic coastal plains, and non-overexploited central plateau regions (Chhattisgarh, eastern MP).
- **Seasonal Fluctuation**: Typically 2 to 4 meters between pre-monsoon (May) and post-monsoon (November).

### 3. Deep Water Table Zone (10 to 20 mbgl)
- **Geographic Extent**: Weathered hard rock peninsular tracts (Maharashtra, interior Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Bundelkhand).
- **Seasonal Fluctuation**: High fluctuation (4 to 9 meters). Water levels drop sharply as summer pumping exhausts shallow weathered aquifers.

### 4. Severely Depleted Deep Water Table Zone (> 20 to 60+ mbgl)
- **Geographic Extent**:
  - **Arid Western India**: Western Rajasthan (Bikaner, Jaisalmer, Barmer, Jodhpur) where regional static water levels exceed 40 to 80 mbgl in deep fossil aquifers.
  - **Intensive Agricultural Irrigation Belts**: Central Punjab (Sangrur, Barnala, Moga, Patiala), central Haryana (Kurukshetra, Kaithal), and North Gujarat (Mehsana, Patan) where water tables have fallen below 30–50 mbgl due to persistent overdraft.
  - **Southern Hard Rock Urban Environs**: Deep borewells in Kolar, Chikkaballapur, and Bengaluru outskirts tapping fracture zones at 150 to 300+ mbgl.
- **Seasonal Fluctuation**: Negligible natural replenishment in deep confined aquifers; slow recovery requires artificial deep injection or years of sustained recharge.
