# Atal Bhujal Yojana (ATAL JAL) Policy and Implementation Guidelines

## 1. Scheme Architecture and Objectives
Atal Bhujal Yojana (ATAL JAL) is a landmark Central Sector Scheme launched by the Ministry of Jal Shakti, Government of India, with financial co-support from the World Bank. The total outlay is INR 6,000 Crore (approximately USD 1 Billion) implemented over a 5-year timeframe.

The overarching objective of ATAL JAL is to improve groundwater management through community-led participatory approaches and targeted demand-side interventions in identified water-stressed priority areas of India.

## 2. Priority Geographic Coverage
The scheme covers 80 priority districts, comprising 229 administrative blocks/talukas and approximately 8,350 Gram Panchayats across seven states representing diverse hydrogeological contexts:
1. **Gujarat**: Covering over-exploited alluvial and hard rock areas in North Gujarat and Saurashtra.
2. **Haryana**: Addressing severely depleted agricultural belts in the Indo-Gangetic alluvium.
3. **Karnataka**: Targeting fractured hard-rock granite and gneiss aquifers in southern and central peninsular districts.
4. **Madhya Pradesh**: Focus on Bundelkhand and Malwa plateau crystalline and basaltic formations.
5. **Maharashtra**: Overcoming water stress in drought-prone basaltic trap zones (Vidarbha and Marathwada).
6. **Rajasthan**: Arid and semi-arid zones facing extreme over-exploitation and falling water tables.
7. **Uttar Pradesh**: Tackling depleted blocks across Bundelkhand and western alluvial agricultural basins.

## 3. Two Core Pillars of Intervention

### Pillar A: Institutional Strengthening and Capacity Building (Component 1)
- Establishing water budgeting and monitoring infrastructure at the Gram Panchayat level.
- Equipping communities with digital water level indicators, rain gauges, and groundwater testing kits.
- Preparing community-driven Water Security Plans (WSPs) vetted by Gram Sabhas.
- Training Jal Sahelis, Bhujal Jaankars (groundwater barefoot technicians), and village water sanitation committees (VWSCs).

### Pillar B: Incentivizing Behavioral and Demand-Side Interventions (Component 2 - Disbursement Linked Indicators)
Unlike traditional capital works, funds under ATAL JAL are disbursed to participating states upon achievement of verifiable Disbursement Linked Indicators (DLIs):
1. **DLI 1**: Public disclosure of groundwater data and information sharing.
2. **DLI 2**: Preparation and approval of community Water Security Plans.
3. **DLI 3**: Convergence of central and state scheme funds (e.g., MGNREGA, PMKSY, RKVY) to finance water harvesting and recharge structures.
4. **DLI 4**: Adoption of efficient water-use practices (drip and sprinkler micro-irrigation, underground pipe systems, laser land leveling, and crop diversification away from water-thirsty crops like paddy and sugarcane).
5. **DLI 5**: Measurable stabilization or deceleration in the rate of groundwater depletion.

## 4. Synergy with Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM)
Atal Bhujal Yojana directly underpins source sustainability for the Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM), which guarantees functional household tap water connections (Har Ghar Jal) to every rural household. Ensuring the long-term hydrological sustainability of tube-wells and borewells powering rural supply systems is the fundamental mission of ATAL JAL.
