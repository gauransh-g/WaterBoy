# Decadal Groundwater Resource Assessment Trends in India (2015–2025)

## 1. Executive Overview and Decadal Trajectory
Over the decadal period spanning 2015 to 2025, India's dynamic groundwater resources have been evaluated through successive national compilations by the Central Ground Water Board (CGWB), Department of Water Resources, River Development and Ganga Rejuvenation, Ministry of Jal Shakti, Government of India. The assessments follow the Ground Water Estimation Committee (GEC-2015) methodology, transitioning from multi-year periodic releases (2013, 2017, 2020) to annual assessments from 2022 onwards (2021-2022, 2022-2023, 2023-2024, and 2024-2025).

### National Macro-Trends (10-Year Comparison: 2015 to 2025)
1. **Total Annual Ground Water Recharge**:
   - 2017 Assessment: 432.00 Billion Cubic Meters (BCM).
   - 2020 Assessment: 436.00 BCM.
   - 2022 Assessment (2021-2022): 437.60 BCM.
   - 2023 Assessment (2022-2023): 449.08 BCM.
   - 2024 Assessment (2023-2024): 449.62 BCM.
   - 2025 Assessment (2024-2025): 451.20 BCM.
   - Decadal Net Shift: An overall increase of approximately 19 BCM in annual replenishment, largely propelled by state-level artificial recharge interventions, canal seepage management, and community water harvesting structures (Mission Amrit Sarovar).

2. **Total Annual Ground Water Extraction (Draft)**:
   - 2017 Assessment: 249.00 BCM.
   - 2020 Assessment: 244.92 BCM.
   - 2022 Assessment: 239.16 BCM.
   - 2023 Assessment: 241.34 BCM.
   - 2024 Assessment: 242.06 BCM.
   - 2025 Assessment: 243.10 BCM.
   - Sectoral Usage: Agriculture accounts for ~87% of cumulative annual groundwater draft, domestic consumption accounts for ~11%, and industrial activities represent ~2%.

3. **National Stage of Ground Water Extraction (SOE)**:
   - 2017: 63.3%.
   - 2020: 61.6%.
   - 2022: 60.08%.
   - 2023: 59.23%.
   - 2024: 59.18%.
   - 2025: 58.95%.
   - National status remains in the "Safe" category overall (< 70%), but with extreme geographic disparity between surplus eastern states and severely depleted northwestern and southern hard rock regions.

## 2. Decadal Categorization of Assessment Units
Out of approximately 6,550 to 7,021 assessed administrative assessment units (blocks, mandals, talukas, firkas, watersheds):
- **Safe Units (SOE <= 70%)**: Increased from 63% in 2017 to over 67% in 2024–2025.
- **Semi-Critical Units (70% < SOE <= 90%)**: Hover around 11% to 12% nationally.
- **Critical Units (90% < SOE <= 100%)**: Account for ~3% to 4% of assessment units.
- **Over-Exploited Units (SOE > 100%)**: Decreased from 17.2% in 2017 to approximately 14% to 15% in 2024–2025. Notable recovery has been observed in parts of Gujarat, Western Madhya Pradesh, and Tamil Nadu due to watershed development.
- **Saline Units**: Represent ~1% to 1.5% of units, predominantly located in inland arid Rajasthan, coastal Gujarat, and southwest Haryana.

## 3. Decadal Trajectories Across Critical Indian States

### Punjab and Haryana (Indo-Gangetic Intensive Irrigation Belt)
- **10-Year Trajectory**: Highest groundwater stress in India.
- **Punjab**: Average stage of extraction remains between 160% and 165% across 2015–2025. Over 75% of blocks are categorized as Over-Exploited due to intensive paddy-wheat monoculture, free or subsidized agricultural electricity, and deep tubewell pumping from alluvial aquifers.
- **Haryana**: Average extraction stage has stabilized between 130% and 135%. Southern and central districts (Kurukshetra, Kaithal, Mahendragarh) remain heavily depleted, while salinity is prevalent in southwest tracts (Sirsa, Fatehabad).

### Rajasthan (Arid Alluvial and Hard Rock Region)
- **10-Year Trajectory**: Average stage of extraction ranges between 145% and 150%.
- Over 85% of assessed blocks are Over-Exploited or Critical. Scant rainfall (< 300 to 500 mm/year), deep water tables exceeding 40 to 60 meters below ground level (mbgl), and limited natural recharge make Rajasthan dependent on fossil groundwater reserves.

### Delhi (National Capital Territory)
- **10-Year Trajectory**: Stage of extraction improved slightly from 120% in 2017 to 95–98% in 2022–2024, driven by mandatory rainwater harvesting in urban bylaws, rejuvenation of water bodies, and piped surface water augmentation from Yamuna and upper Ganga canal networks.
- South, Southwest, and New Delhi districts remain Over-Exploited, whereas East Delhi and Yamuna flood plains remain Safe.

### Maharashtra and Karnataka (Deccan Trap Basalt and Peninsular Granite)
- **Maharashtra**: Average extraction stage is between 52% and 55% (Safe overall), but intense seasonal localized depletion occurs in rain-shadow districts (Marathwada, Ahmednagar, Solapur) due to water-intensive sugarcane cultivation.
- **Karnataka**: Average stage is ~68–70%. Depletion is concentrated in the southern crystalline hard rock plateau (Kolar, Chikkaballapur, Bengaluru Urban/Rural) with tubewells exceeding 250–350 meters depth.

### Eastern & North-Eastern States (Assam, Bihar, West Bengal, Odisha)
- **10-Year Trajectory**: Immense unutilized groundwater potential ("Groundwater Abundant Zone").
- **Assam & NE States**: Stage of extraction < 20% (Safe).
- **Bihar & West Bengal**: Stage of extraction ~45% to 55% (Safe). High monsoon rainfall and thick Gangetic alluvial aquifers ensure massive annual replenishment, though water quality concerns (Arsenic and Iron) pose critical potable water challenges.
